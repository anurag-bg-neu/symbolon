The basics, one question at a time.

What is a carrier?

You have two copies of almost every gene, one from each parent. A carrier has one copy that doesn't work and one that does.

Carriers are healthy. Most people carry a few of these changes and never know it.

Why does it matter for couples?

Some conditions only affect a child who gets a non-working copy from both parents. If both partners carry the same gene, each pregnancy has a 25% (1 in 4) chance of a child being affected.

That is a chance, not a certainty. Options exist, and a genetic counselor can explain them.

How does GeneMatch keep it private?

Picture each of you putting a padlock on every item on your list, then swapping lists. Each of you adds a second padlock to the other's items.

An item with both padlocks looks the same no matter whose lock went on first. So matching items line up, and nothing is ever unlocked. Your list stays on your device, and the server in the middle only ever sees locked items.

What can I do with it?
  • Couple check. Learn whether you share a carrier gene, either how many or which ones, as you both agree.
  • Trial eligibility. Show a clinical trial one lab-verified finding, and nothing else.
  • Later. The same idea could help with donor matching or community screening.
What GeneMatch doesn't do
  • It doesn't diagnose anything, and it isn't medical advice.
  • It never tells anyone whether to marry or have children.
  • "No match" means lower risk for the conditions tested, not zero risk. Conditions that weren't tested aren't covered.

A couple check, in four steps.

Meet Alex and Sam. They're synthetic, and so are their results.

  1. 1. Results arrive from the lab.

    Alex's wallet holds three carrier results, Sam's holds two. The lab signs them, so nobody can fake them.

  2. 2. They agree what to learn.

    Alex sends Sam an invite code. Sam sees exactly what they'll both learn before saying yes.

  3. 3. Lists are locked and swapped.

    Each wallet locks its list and they trade. Each adds a second lock, so matching items line up.

  4. 4. They see only the answer.

    Both learn they share one carrier gene. Neither sees the other's full list, and the server sees neither.
